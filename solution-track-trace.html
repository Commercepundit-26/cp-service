Real-world risks

The Cost of Not Knowing

These aren't inconveniences. They're the moments a visibility gap turns into a line item on a board deck.

01
A defect just surfaced in one batch. How much of your inventory do you have to recall?
Without unit-level precision, one bad batch forces a blanket recall across the entire product line, at a cost that scales into millions, not thousands.
02
Legal needs proof of custody from eight months ago. A regulator wants it right now.
Reconstructing that history across disconnected systems takes weeks, and a regulator won't wait: the delay alone can suspend a license or block a shipment.
03
Finance can't close the books because $2M in inventory is stuck in unresolved distributor disputes.
When "shipped" and "received" can't be proven to match, disputed shipments sit unresolved for months, tying up real working capital.
04
One warehouse is sitting on six months of overstock while another can't keep a single SKU in stock.
Without visibility into what's actually moving, overstock and understock pile up in the same network at the same time.
05
Nobody can say which SKUs are fast-moving, slow-moving, or already dead stock until the write-off shows up.
Shipment data shows what left the plant, not what's moving off the shelf, so all three look identical until the capital's already lost.
